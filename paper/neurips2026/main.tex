\begin{abstract}
EEG affect systems may benefit from labelled user calibration and repeated
stimuli as well as EEG. A higher system score therefore does not, by itself,
establish incremental value from EEG. We introduce OpenAffect-EEG, an evaluation
method that fixes test trials, varies participant-calibration and
repeated-stimulus label doses, preserves population-training size, and compares
EEG predictors with EEG-free priors receiving the same labels. A complementary
execution-contract checker tests observable resource parity, data lineage, and
deployment claims. In a bounded sensitivity analysis on EmoEEG-MC and urban-image
appraisal, two settings each of band-power regression, frozen LaBraM, and
standard EEGNet cover complete dose grids. Label resources consistently improve
the EEG-free prior, whereas all mean matched EEG-increment intervals include
zero. This is not evidence of equivalence or absent EEG information: it limits
attribution of total system gains. Executed paths from two pinned external
toolkits illustrate why split names and trial-intersection checks do not by
themselves certify broader evaluation contracts. We retain earlier auditory,
condition-level, and representation audits, including uncertain results.
The contribution is a reproducible comparison object and checkable evidence
for its assumptions, not a new encoder or a latent-emotion decomposition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An EEG emotion system can use more than the current EEG recording. It may have
ratings from a target user's previous trials, ratings of the same stimulus from
other users, or source-released normative stimulus scores. These resources are
legitimate when available at deployment. The evaluation problem arises when an
EEG system receives them but its EEG-free comparator does not, or when a
protocol name conceals their availability. Canonical affect datasets contain
repeated stimuli and individual self-reports, making this distinction concrete
\citep{koelstra2012deap,daly2020musiceeg,openneuro_ds005540}.

Existing research establishes the importance of trial-level leakage prevention,
participant and stimulus holdout, target-user calibration, and identity audits
\citep{brookshire2024leakage,lei2025leakage,bendrich2022feature,bhosale2022calibration,lin2026identitytrap}.
Our question is narrower: \emph{on the same test trials, what changes when the
EEG and EEG-free predictors are given the same participant and stimulus label
resources?} A protocol can exclude every test trial and still leave this
comparison unmatched. Conversely, resource matching alone does not establish
generalization to identities that have been observed during calibration.

We distinguish three score differences: total system gain against an
uncalibrated prior, calibration gain available without EEG, and the remaining
EEG difference against that calibrated comparator. The point is not that
subtraction is new, or that more labels can help. It is that the comparison
requires an explicit, executable resource design: fixed test support, controlled
population-training size, identical calibration labels, and declared external
priors. The same design also specifies which deployment claims can be checked.
We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A resource-matched evaluation object.} A fixed-support response
surface independently doses target-participant calibration and other-participant
ratings of test stimuli, with training-row replacement and paired EEG-free
controls. We define its estimand and assumptions rather than claiming causal
identity isolation.
\item \textbf{Evidence about attribution under controlled resources.} Complete
grids and bounded training sensitivity show how favourable total system gains
can coexist with uncertain matched EEG increments. Earlier models, auditory
results, and unsupported comparisons remain available instead of being replaced
by a favourable subset.
\item \textbf{Reusable checks of observable execution contracts.} Trial lineage,
predictor resources, preprocessing and selection roles, and fixed test hashes
produce allow, block, or unverifiable decisions. Pinned external code paths and
separately identified synthetic faults document what these checks do and do
not establish.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Relation to Existing Evaluation Work}
EEGain standardizes datasets, preprocessing, models, splits, metrics, and
prediction logging \citep{kukhilava2025eegain}. FMScope audits frozen
representations with subject-axis, spectral, layer, and within-subject
diagnostics \citep{lin2026identitytrap}. Both address genuine problems;
neither is a weak baseline to be defeated by adding more features to a checker.

observable inputs, not by an unsupported claim that no related method exists.

Calibration, joint person--stimulus holdout, and EEG toolboxes are established
\citep{li2021calibration,bendrich2022feature,liu2026eegemolib}. Likewise,
identity probes, linear erasure, data cards, and provenance hashes are not
claimed as individually novel
\citep{lin2026identitytrap,gebru2021datasheets,mitchell2019modelcards,akhtar2024croissant}.
Outside EEG, supervision-conditioned benchmarking, controlled metric stress
tests, and documented benchmark criteria are also established
\citep{han2022adbench,liu2024tsbad,reuel2024betterbench}. Dose curves,
simulation, and checklists are therefore not separate novelty claims here.
Our scope is resource-conditioned evaluation of observed self-reports. It is
not the physiological mechanism audited by FMScope, nor a claim to exceed the
dataset coverage or classification results of EEGain.

\section{Resource-Matched Evaluation}
\subsection{Trial graph and resource contract}
Each trial has a participant identity $i$, stimulus identity $j$, observed
report $y$, context, and source lineage. Windows inherit their complete trial's
partition. Let $T_b$ be the fixed test trials in identity block $b$ and
$D_b^{p,s}$ the population development data at doses $(p,s)$. Let $C_{i,b}^p$
contain $p$ labelled calibration trials from target participant $i$, on stimuli
outside the test stimulus set. Repeated-stimulus dose $s$ counts population
ratings per test stimulus from other participants. We record a contract
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_b(p,s)=
\bigl(T_b,D_b^{p,s},\{C_{i,b}^p\}_i,N_b,A_b,F_b,V_b\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $N_b$ is the fixed population-training row count, $A_b$ lists additional
label resources such as normative scores, and $F_b,V_b$ record preprocessing
fit and model-selection trials. This notation describes actual supplied
resources; it is not an identity-information measure.

Repeated-stimulus rows replace context- and target-matched training donors, so
population row counts remain fixed. Calibration rows do not fit the population
model; they estimate a post-fit participant mean residual offset. Dose sets
are deterministic and nested. Removing donors changes training composition;
we control count and approximate label/context balance, not every property of
the training distribution. Nor is total labelled-data budget fixed across the
participant axis: additional calibration is the resource being measured.

The principal invariants are: no test trial in fit, selection, or calibration;
identical test trials across cells and models; exact realised doses; no target
participant in population fitting; and identical non-EEG labels for paired
predictors. Test identities are initially jointly held out, but at nonzero
doses the corresponding identities have been exposed. Released normative
scores, where available, remain separately declared at zero stimulus-label
dose. Zero dose therefore does not generally mean no stimulus knowledge.
Unverifiable session, device, or upstream pretraining identities cannot support
a strict isolation certificate.

\subsection{Predictors and the estimand}
The population prior $P$ uses fit-set stimulus means, falling back to declared
source normative ratings or the fit-global mean. $PC$ adds the target user's
mean calibration residual without EEG. $E$ adds an EEG-predicted population
residual to $P$, and $EC$ calibrates $E$ using exactly the same trial labels as
$PC$. Both calibrated predictors use the same mean-offset rule. The residual
is arithmetic relative to the specified prior, not an identified private
emotion. Direct EEG prediction is retained as a secondary control.

% evidence: src/openaffect_eeg/evidence_argument.py; src/openaffect_eeg/exposure_statistics.py
For score functional $Q$ on the same $T_b$, define
\begin{align}
G_{\rm total}(p,s)&=Q(EC)-Q(P),\\
G_{\rm cal}(p,s)&=Q(PC)-Q(P),\\
\Delta_{\rm EEG}(p,s)&=Q(EC)-Q(PC).
\label{eq:matched-increment}
\end{align}
Thus $G_{\rm total}=G_{\rm cal}+\Delta_{\rm EEG}$ exactly. This is a
telescoping score identity, not an additive decomposition of CCC into neural
mechanisms. $G_{\rm total}$ can assess an entire system package; it cannot
isolate EEG added value when calibration resources differ. At $p=0$, $PC=P$
and the matched contrast reduces to $Q(E)-Q(P)$.

% evidence: configs/final_robustness_v9.json
Our primary summary is the uniform mean of $\Delta_{\rm EEG}$ in CCC over
five fixed identity blocks and the complete grid $\{0,1,2,4,8\}^2$.
We report paired MAE differences as a secondary metric; positive MAE differences
mean greater error. Uniform grid weighting is a specified benchmark utility,
not an estimated deployment-frequency distribution. Endpoint tables illustrate
comparison choice but do not replace this primary summary. Every grid cell is
retained in the aggregate release.

\subsection{Observable execution checks}
The checker consumes a trial-identity table, an execution record, and boolean
deployment requirements. Unlike a split-name lookup, it compares executed UID
sets for population labels, calibration labels, test support, preprocessing,
and model selection. Required missing evidence yields \texttt{unverifiable},
not a pass. A detected violation yields \texttt{block}; otherwise supplied
observable evidence may \texttt{allow} the requested checks. Source identity
authenticity and upstream pretraining exposure remain unverifiable even when
observable checks pass.

For example, a calibrated target-user system can pass test-trial isolation yet
fail a requested unseen-participant contract. Two predictors can also share
every train/test split but fail resource parity because only one receives
calibration labels. These are different checks with different inputs. A basic
intersection checker is not expected to infer undeclared resource use. The
resource checker complements it; it is not a universally more accurate detector.

\section{Experimental Evidence}
\subsection{Coverage and bounded training sensitivity}
% evidence: paper/generated/final_closure_v9/verification/verification.json; configs/final_robustness_v9.json
The latest coverage analysis uses EmoEEG-MC (30 eligible participants, 42
stimuli; valence and arousal) and Urban Appraisal (62 participants, 56 street
images; genuine valence trials only)
\citep{openneuro_ds005540,openneuro_ds006850}.
Deterministic participant and stimulus blocks test every eligible identity
once, producing 252 and 695 test trials respectively. Only diagonal identity
blocks are tested, not all possible pairs. Urban is an external appraisal
task, not an identical replication of the emotion-induction paradigm. These
data have been used in earlier analyses; the new blocks are not an untouched
confirmatory cohort.

% evidence: configs/final_robustness_v9.json; paper/generated/final_closure_v9/verification/verification.json
Each task includes band-power Ridge, frozen LaBraM, and the standard
Braindecode EEGNet \citep{jiang2024labram,lawhern2018eegnet}, each with two bounded settings, across five blocks and all
25 cells: 1,500 model-cell results in total, not independent observations.
Ridge settings vary the candidate regularization grid; EEGNet settings vary
learning rate. Validation selects hyperparameters and training duration;
population refitting combines training and validation, never test or
calibration trials. The direct and residual EEGNet branches have 200 recorded
selection/refit learning histories. They are finite and nonconstant, which
checks execution but does not establish optimal training. Full settings,
coverage, preprocessing, and historical model panels appear in the Appendix.

\subsection{Paired uncertainty}
% evidence: paper/generated/final_closure_v9/percentile/added_value_statistics.json; paper/generated/final_closure_v9/normal_t/added_value_statistics.json
Crossed bootstrap applies the same participant and stimulus multiplicities to
all paired predictors, cells, and blocks. Of 2,000 draws, 1,985 are valid for
EmoEEG-MC and 2,000 for Urban. We report percentile intervals and retain an
explicitly heuristic small-cluster t-normal sensitivity analysis. Intervals
condition on the fixed fitted predictions; they do not include full retraining
uncertainty. Fixed blocks and settings are not independent study replications.
Small-cluster coverage diagnostics remain in the release, and nominal coverage
is not guaranteed. We distinguish pointwise intervals from separately supplied
dose-family simultaneous bands; neither corrects all model comparisons.

\paragraph{Stress-testing the evaluator.}
Before interpreting observed data, we pass the same matched-contrast analyzer
through prespecified Gaussian crossed simulations with analytic population CCC.
Across 16 trial-signal settings (100 replicates each), positive-detection rates
are 0.85--1.00 and pointwise coverage is 0.92--1.00. A labels-only predictor
gives exact zero, and no positive detection is observed in 16 independent-noise
settings; the latter is finite simulation evidence, not a zero false-positive
rate. Fresh 300-replicate diagnostics give 0.933 percentile coverage in the
previous 32-participant/24-stimulus problem case, but the minimum falls to 0.837
with 8 participants and 6 stimuli. We therefore validate the comparison's
intended behavior while retaining small-support interval failure as a limit;
Appendix~\ref{app:evaluator-validation} reports all scenario roles and methods.

\subsection{What the matched comparator changes}

Table~\ref{tab:resource-decomposition} retains every setting at the same
predeclared maximum dose. For EmoEEG-MC LaBraM R1, total gain relative to $P$
is $+0.0961$, while the EEG-free calibration gain is $+0.1420$. The matched EEG

The full-grid primary summaries in Table~\ref{tab:primary-v9} have intervals
including zero for all 12 task--setting combinations under both interval
methods. No equivalence margin was prespecified. These findings therefore
limit claims of a stable average positive increment in the evaluated settings;
they do not establish equivalence or a ceiling on future EEG methods.
The secondary MAE contrast for LaBraM R1 is positive in both tasks under both
interval constructions, indicating increased error for that configuration;
other configurations include uncertain and interval-sensitive effects.
All CCC and MAE settings, rather than selected significant rows, are reported
in Appendix~\ref{app:latest-evidence}.

% evidence: paper/generated/final_closure_v9/normal_t/table_added_value_summary.csv
The EEG-free prior is itself resource-sensitive. From zero doses, increasing
only participant calibration to eight trials changes $PC$ CCC by $+0.2036$
in EmoEEG-MC and $+0.1564$ in Urban; changing only stimulus-label dose to eight
changes it by $+0.4907$ and $+0.3408$. The corresponding percentile and
t-normal intervals are positive. This is not novel evidence that labels help.
It explains why a non-EEG resource comparator is necessary when interpreting
the much smaller matched EEG contrasts.

\subsection{Earlier evidence remains part of the record}
The original EmoEEG-MC and MusicEEG surfaces, standard versus compact EEGNet,
CBraMod, LaBraM, same-architecture random controls, frozen EMOD, and
shrinkage-calibration analyses remain in Appendix~\ref{app:exposure-surface}.
Their support and protocols are distinct from the latest coverage blocks and
must not be pooled as repeated measurements of the same experiment.
% evidence: paper/generated/competitive_revision_v3/table_provenance.json
The earlier MusicEEG LaBraM uniform-grid increment is
$\VThreeMusicMean$ [$\VThreeMusicLow,\VThreeMusicHigh$], an uncertain
auditory case. We retain it rather than use Urban to erase a non-replicating
result. Validation-frozen resource-conditioned model selection also lacks a
stable mean CCC benefit (Appendix~\ref{app:selection-utility}); conditional
ranking summaries are not evidence of a proven deployment-selection advantage.

Identity probes and subspace perturbations remain representation diagnostics,
not causal evidence. The ds006866 extension concerns conditions, not unknown
image identities. DENS is missingness-limited; SEED-V is an authorized
supplement, not an additional openly redistributed benchmark. EMOD's repaired
single-seed learning check is distinct from its frozen audit and from complete
original-task replication; mdJPT has no completed numerical audit here.
These boundaries restrict model and domain coverage without making positive
EEG performance a prerequisite for an evaluation contribution.

\paragraph{Reusable interface.}
The source exposes the following no-EEG workflow audit:
\begin{small}\begin{verbatim}
python -m openaffect_eeg.workflow_audit \
  --trials trials.tsv --execution execution.json \
  --contract contract.json --output report.json
\end{verbatim}\end{small}
The report names failed checks and counts set differences. Reproduction
instructions include a synthetic example with matched, unmatched, missing
provenance, and falsely claimed unseen-user cases. Separate raw-data commands
run model experiments on user-provided licensed data. File hashes establish
byte lineage, not the truth of a supplied UID or upstream training history.
The release also contains a machine-readable rule catalog and a source-linked
claim-repair record: across five EEGain folds, the same pinned split function
and trial universe block a joint subject-plus-validation-trial claim, while the
narrower held-out-test-participant claim is allowed. This repairs the scope of
the claim; it does not repair or invalidate an external model score.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\paragraph{The judgement corrected.}
We correct an \emph{attribution rule}: an EEG system's advantage over an
uncalibrated prior is not, without resource parity, its incremental EEG value.
The experiments show this distinction is numerically material in the evaluated
tasks. They do not establish that earlier publications actually used that
unmatched comparison. Nor does every existing subject-independent protocol
become invalid: a properly scoped zero-calibration claim remains meaningful.
The proposed resource ledger refines that claim rather than dismissing it.

\paragraph{When is the method useful?}
A researcher comparing encoders can hold test and labelled resources fixed; a
system designer can report a user-calibration budget alongside performance;
and a reproducer can distinguish a valid trial split from a resource-matched
comparison. These are supported workflow capabilities. We have not established
broad adoption, independently measured usability, improved model selection, or
an economic benefit from EEG acquisition. A paired no-EEG comparator is a
baseline for the stated task, not a clinically validated alternative device.

\paragraph{Limits of the empirical claim.}
The latest analysis is retrospective, covers selected identity pairs, and uses
bounded training settings. Frozen representations and post-fit calibration do
not span all fine-tuning or adaptation methods. Learned preprocessing excludes
test trials, but stimulus-dose injection can change validation-side identity
relations; we do not claim every validation identity stays isolated. The
participant and stimulus dose axes are resource interventions on a trial graph,
not randomized interventions on latent affect. Extra normative labels and
unverifiable pretraining exposure must remain visible in the contract.
Uniform-grid averages can obscure heterogeneous cells, hence the complete
release; intervals condition on fixed fits and have small-cluster limitations.
The independently collected tasks differ in elicitation and target definition,
so they do not establish universal EEG-emotion behaviour.

\section{Artifacts, Ethics, and Conclusion}
Code, aggregate results, split rules, source versions, model/data cards,
synthetic examples, and evidence hashes accompany the work. An anonymity-scanned,
hash-manifested review archive is built with the manuscript; its exact archive
must be placed at the accessible code URL supplied to reviewers. Developer-run
clean-environment acceptance is reported separately from independent-human
acceptance, which is not claimed. EEG recordings, derived individual features,
trial-level private predictions, stimulus materials, and model weights are not
redistributed. Original consent and licensing apply; Urban's snapshot contains
inconsistent license wording, documented without redistributing its recordings.
Identity probes must not be used to identify participants, and self-report
prediction is not access to a clinical state or private emotion.

OpenAffect-EEG provides a fixed-support, resource-matched comparison and an
executable check of its observable assumptions. Its main finding concerns
what a score contrast warrants: total system improvement and EEG added value
must be reported separately. Uncertain matched increments are retained as
uncertain; the contribution is improved evaluation specificity and
reproducibility, not a requirement that EEG either succeed or fail.
