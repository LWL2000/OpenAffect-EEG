# Novelty and practical cases, scoped to existing evidence

This note distinguishes the contribution from the nearest prior work. It is not
an exhaustive priority claim and does not assert that every individual design
element is new.

## The additional evaluative object

Participant label-tendency baselines and identity-confounding critiques already
exist. In particular, Pandilova et al. (2026), *Subject Identity Confounds qEEG
Emotion Recognition on DEAP and DREAMER*, evaluate an identity-only comparator
on matched folds and rows and report matched personalization comparisons. That
paper must be credited, not treated as if no-EEG controls were invented here.

OpenAffect-EEG's tested addition is the conjunction of:

- Independent target-participant and repeated-stimulus label-dose axes.
- Identical test support and paired outputs, with constant population-training
  row count while shared stimulus-label resources change.
- A declared system/calibration/matched-EEG score contrast.
- Executed resource records and a checker that distinguishes split validity,
  resource parity and deployment-claim support.

Equal training size does not guarantee identical training composition. Equal
labels do not imply equal optimization. The design identifies the numerical
contrast for a declared learning pipeline and resource contract, not an optimal
information bound or a causal physiological mechanism.

## Existing cases that show a consequence

1. **Attribution changes in two development tasks.** At the fixed maximum dose,
   LaBraM R1 total CCC gains are +0.0961 / +0.0587, while the same-resource
   no-EEG calibration gains are +0.1420 / +0.0774. The matched differences are
   -0.0459 / -0.0186. These are point contrasts; neither the total nor matched
   endpoint ranges establishes a positive gain. They do not invalidate an
   external paper or prove EEG acquisition is economically unnecessary.
2. **The shared stimulus budget changes what the contrast addresses.** On
   FACED, LaBraM's participant-dose-zero point increment is +0.0170 at stimulus
   dose zero and -0.0212 at dose eight. The entire grid, its two architectures,
   all four corners and simultaneous bands are retained. The point changes are
   descriptive; cell-level equivalence and acquisition-policy superiority are
   not established. A participant-only baseline does not describe that
   stimulus-resource surface.
3. **A source-linked execution claim narrows under inspection.** The same pinned
   EEGain split path blocks the conjunction of held-out test participants and
   train/validation trial isolation in five folds, while allowing the narrower
   test-participant isolation claim in all five. Marker windows are lineage
   stand-ins, not new EEG. This is a checkable execution-scope repair; the
   original classifier scores are not reproduced or invalidated.

## Transfer and unresolved scientific limits

The cross-domain resource-contract argument is supported by primary literature
on multimodal ablations, controlled vision--language comparisons, active
learning budgets, domain adaptation and personalized federated learning.
It is an evaluation template and literature-supported motivation, not a new
empirical demonstration outside EEG. No new cross-domain experiment is claimed.

Two failed locked sinusoidal controls remain failed. Post-failure signed-power
recovery narrows the software diagnosis but cannot validate all realistic EEG
signals or restore full preregistration. FACED average equivalence is bounded
by the declared model, targets, grid and margins. The current evidence supports
changing the evaluation question and comparator; it does not establish universal
EEG uselessness, model-ranking superiority, broad adoption or cost savings.
